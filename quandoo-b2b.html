TL;DR

What we built

A redesign of the Quandoo Business Center, the web hub restaurant owners use to manage reservations, customers and daily performance.

How we built it

Interviews and observation with real merchants, A/B comparison tests, and continuous feedback loops with product, development and merchant operations.

What we learned

+15% bookings, +30% time efficiency and +40% user satisfaction, driven by clearer navigation, an insight-first dashboard and a better reservation flow.

User problem
Running a restaurant is already a juggling act. Behind the scenes, owners were losing time to a tangled navigation, burying key metrics under a mountain of data, and wrestling with a cluttered reservation system, all while service was happening.
Context
The Quandoo Business Center is the web application built for restaurant owners to orchestrate reservations, customer interactions and business performance. The redesign was grounded in merchant feedback, clear business objectives and technical realities.
My responsibility
I was Lead Product Designer: desk research, design, design documentation and handoff, working with the Product Lead, the Development Lead and the Merchants & Operations team.
What we did
We simplified navigation, redesigned the dashboard to surface essential metrics at a glance, and rebuilt reservation management around a fluid drag-and-drop calendar.

Outcomes

What changed once the redesigned Business Center was in the hands of real restaurant owners:

+15% bookings, a direct result of a more intuitive reservation management system (tracked through internal booking data)
+30% time efficiency when managing daily operations (confirmed through user surveys and timed task analysis in usability sessions)
+40% user satisfaction, consistently reflected in post-redesign interviews and satisfaction surveys

Challenges and takeaways

Data overload, insight underload. Owners needed to read the pulse of the business in seconds, but the numbers that mattered were buried. The challenge was deciding what to surface first, and letting colour and clear graphs do the work.

One tool, three very different days. A manager on the floor, an owner watching revenue and a staff member checking the schedule all use the same app. Continuous feedback loops with merchants kept each of them honestly served.

Phase 01

Research

Finding out what wasn't working, straight from the people running the kitchens.

We knew the existing web app had its quirks, making it harder than it should be for restaurant owners to run their show. The first step was to dig into what wasn't working through desk research, deep-dive interviews with restaurant owners and staff, and observation sessions where we watched people use the existing app in real time.

Key Learning 1
A tangled web of navigation. Users often felt lost moving between parts of the app, which made simple tasks feel complicated.
Key Learning 2
Data overload, insight underload. Important business metrics were buried under a mountain of information.
Key Learning 3
Cluttered reservation chaos. Managing reservations felt messy and confusing, adding stress to an already busy environment.

Through conversations and observation we also pinned down the daily pain points: time sinks (too much time spent wrestling with reservations and digging for statistics), the frustration factor (confusing layouts and tricky processes) and one-size-fits-none data (users wanted more control over how they viewed and managed it).

Phase 03

Test

Putting our ideas in front of merchants, and letting the data guide the decisions.

To make sure the design solutions hit the mark, we kept continuous feedback loops running through the project, like fine-tuning an instrument: each iteration made the interface more harmonious.

  • 01Deep-dive interviews: in-depth conversations with restaurant owners and staff about their daily struggles and what they truly wished for in the app.
  • 02Observational insights: watching users interact with the existing app in real time to pinpoint exactly where confusion and frustration happened.
  • 03A/B comparison tests: direct comparisons between design approaches, so data decided which elements were most effective and user-friendly.
  • 04Usability sessions and surveys: timed task analysis and post-redesign surveys and interviews to confirm the impact.

Phase 04

Design

Three core solutions, each answering a pain point we heard directly from merchants.

Clearer pathways

A more intuitive menu structure, so owners and staff reach the features they need with significantly fewer clicks.

  • Simplified information architecture
  • Fewer clicks to everyday tasks

The insightful dashboard

A completely redesigned main dashboard that presents the essential metrics at a glance, so complex data becomes easy to understand and act on.

  • Essential metrics first
  • Smart colour coding
  • Clear, readable graphs

Effortless reservation flow

A complete overhaul of reservation management, built around a fluid drag-and-drop calendar that makes creating and managing bookings intuitive and efficient.

  • Drag-and-drop calendar
  • Faster booking creation and changes

This experience was a powerful reminder of empathy's role in design: truly understanding your users is the secret to building successful products.
